# A formally verified lower bound for the proportion of simple critical-line zeros of ζ

Rishi Khan and Albert DeWeese. The paper is `paper/simple-zeros-lower-bound.pdf`.

The paper proves, unconditionally,

    liminf_{T→∞} N₀ˢ(T,2T) / N(T,2T) ≥ κ_L = 854046308300026565326564954594528531931648
                                              / 1267673952595351605232565361802825927734375
                                            = 0.67371133291…,

where N₀ˢ(T,2T) counts the simple zeros on the critical line with ordinates in (T,2T]. A Lean 4
proof against Mathlib proves this bound end to end (Theorem 1.2). The proof uses only the axioms
`propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## Reproducing each result

`make` with no target lists every target with its cost. `make venv` first installs the pinned
Python dependencies.

| result in the paper | command | cost |
|---|---|---|
| Theorem 1.2 (and Theorem 1.1) at q = 12: build, axioms, statements | `make proof Q=12` | ~30 min, ~15 GB |
| the same, checked by the Lean kernel and by nanoda | `make proof Q=12 PROOF_OPTS=--deep` | ~11 h, ~15 GB |
| Proposition 5.1: the H floor of the window of Table 1 | `make hfloor` | 1 s |
| Proposition 5.1: the window conditions (W1), (W2) | `make conditions` | 2 s |
| Table 2: the Lean proofs at q = 9, 10, 11 | `make proof Q=9`, `Q=10`, `Q=11` (add `PROOF_OPTS=--deep`) | 10 min to 1.5 h each |
| Table 2, q = 9: the entry's Lean source, regenerated byte for byte | `src/lbcert/README.md`, "The q = 9 entry" | 10 to 20 s |
| Appendix C: the search for the window, and a Lean proof from its window | `src/lbcert/README.md` | q = 12: Perlmutter; the window search took about 12 node-hours |
| the PDF | `make paper` | ~1 min, needs `pdflatex` |

`THREADS` (default 8) sets the threads of the Lean build, nanoda and the kernel replay. The
costs above are at `THREADS=8` on a 20-core aarch64 machine with 119 GB of memory.

## The Lean proofs

The generated Lean sources are 2 MB (q = 9), 21 MB (q = 10), 85 MB (q = 11) and 425 MB (q = 12).
They are archived on Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24166562), not in this repository.
`proofs/archives.tsv` lists each archive with its SHA-256 and κ.

    bash proofs/fetch.sh q12             # download, check the hash, unpack into proofs/q12/
    bash proofs/verify.sh q12            # build; check axioms; print the statements
    bash proofs/verify.sh q12 --deep     # also: lean4export, nanoda, parallel Lean kernel replay
    bash proofs/verify.sh q10 --compare  # also: the challenge's comparator, as the hosted verifier runs it

`verify.sh` needs `elan`. `--deep` and `--compare` also need `git` and `cargo`, and build
comparator `2732944` (with lean4export `b18d673`) and nanoda `4183202`, the versions the
riemannzeta.fun verifier pins. Each archive's `logs/` holds the logs of the run of record.

Before it builds, `verify.sh` regenerates the trusted statement layer from `proofs/templates/` and
the κ in `proofs/archives.tsv`. A proof is therefore checked against statements from this
repository, not from its archive. The build prints `#print axioms` for every headline theorem, and
`verify.sh` writes the statements as Lean elaborates them to `proofs/<q>/check/statements.txt`.

- **Default:** the Lean build. Every generated module of the four proofs (the q = 9 solution and
  the Q10, Q11 and Q12 libraries) sets `debug.skipKernelTC` (the challenge allows it), so the build elaborates the proof without the kernel check. It checks the
  axioms and the statements, not the proof.
- **`--deep`:** the kernel check. It exports the theorems with every dependency, then checks the
  export with nanoda, an independent kernel, and with a parallel replay through Lean's kernel
  (`src/lbcert/q12lean/kreplay/PReplay.lean`).
- **`--compare`:** runs the challenge's comparator. It checks that the three template theorems
  have exactly the template's statements and permitted axioms, then runs nanoda and the Lean
  kernel on the solution, both single-threaded. That takes about 20 minutes at q = 9, about 2
  hours at q = 10, and more than a day at q = 12.

Costs of the runs of record (20-core aarch64, 119 GB RAM):

| proof | build | export | nanoda | kernel replay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| q = 9 | 3.6 min | 0.7 min | 7.8 min | 8.8 min |
| q = 10 | 4.2 min | 1.6 min | 8 min, 6.0 GB | 13 min, 8.1 GB |
| q = 11 | 7.6 min | 5.7 min | 36 min, 7.9 GB | 41 min, 10.3 GB |
| q = 12 | 26.4 min | 26.5 min | 6.1 h, 14.4 GB | 3.9 h, 15.0 GB |

Lake builds modules in parallel, and memory grows with `THREADS`. From a fresh archive at
`THREADS=8` on the same machine, `make proof` (Mathlib cache download, the base module, then the
rest) took 11 minutes at q = 9, 14 at q = 10, 14 at q = 11 and 26 at q = 12, using at most about
17 GB. Each unpacked proof needs 9 to 13 GB of disk, mostly its own copy of Mathlib, or about
41 GB for all four. Start with q = 9 or q = 10 to check the toolchain.

The build and comparator logs contain info messages from the Zeta23 library ("Try this: ring_nf",
"The `ring` tactic failed to close the goal"). They come from `ring` calls that fall back to
`ring_nf`, and they are not errors.

The template theorems state the bound for N₀*, the distinct critical-line zeros.
`candidate_simple_critical_line_bound` and `candidate_simple_critical_line_bound_cumulative`, in the
q = 10..12 proofs, state it for N₀ˢ. The q = 9 entry proves the template theorems only.

## Requirements

Python 3.12 or newer (the pinned numpy and scipy need it); `make venv` creates `.venv` from `requirements.txt`, and every target then
uses it. `make conditions` and `make hfloor` need python-flint and mpmath; the generator also needs
numpy, scipy and cma, a C++17 compiler, HiGHS and GMP. The Lean proofs need `elan`, which
downloads the pinned toolchain, and `perl`; `--deep` and `--compare` also need `git` and `cargo`. `make paper` needs
`pdflatex` with `pgfplots`.
